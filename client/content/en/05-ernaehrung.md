Nutrition has to work in everyday life, not just in an app or on a perfect weekly plan. Good decisions become easier when the broad levers are clear: calorie balance, enough protein, fiber-rich foods, suitable carbohydrates, simple beverage decisions and a flexible approach to tracking. This chapter lays the nutritional basis. Measuring methods, formulas and tools are available in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md); Planned fat loss is described in [06 Diet Phase](06-diaetphase.md), controlled weight gain in [07 Mass Phase](07-massephase.md).

## 5.1 Calorie balance controls the weight trend

Body weight changes in the long term primarily through energy balance. Individual foods are rarely the main problem; Quantity, regularity, saturation and environment matter more.

Daily values ​​are too loud for that. Trends over several weeks are more meaningful. How weight, waistline, calories and macros are measured correctly is collected in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md).

## 5.2 Secure protein

For healthy adults aged 19 to under 65, the DGE cites 0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight and day as a reference value; for healthy, normal-weight people aged 65 and over, it gives an estimated value of 1.0 g/kg/day (<a href="https://www.dge.de/presse/meldungen/2020/positionspapier-zur-proteinzufuhr-im-sport/">DGE 2020</a>). These values ​​secure the general basis. They are not automatically optimal target values ​​for strength training, diet, muscle building, high satiety or the best possible muscle maintenance. For athletes who train more than 5 hours per week, the DGE recommends 1.2-2.0 g/kg/day (<a href="https://www.dge.de/presse/meldungen/2020/positionspapier-zur-proteinzufuhr-im-sport/">DGE 2020</a>), depending on the goal and training condition. The ISSN lists 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day as a reasonable range for most exercisers (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>).

**Target areas:**

- **General Health:** 19 to under 65 years about 0.8 g/kg/day; from 65 years of age around 1.0 g/kg/day as a DGE estimate. In practice, these are lower limits for basic care, not the upper limit of what can be useful.
- **Strength training/muscle building:** often 1.6-2.0 g/kg/day.
- **Diet/fat loss:** often 1.8-2.2 g/kg/day, especially with strength training, leaner starting positions and high satiety requirements; Sports-related recommendations for natural bodybuilding diets are in this range, but are not automatically transferable to every normal diet (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24864135/">Helms et al. 2014</a>).
- **Very high values:** only use specifically if nutrition, kidney function, fluid intake and context are appropriate.

If you train, want to lose weight, are older, have little muscle mass or need a high level of satiety, it often makes sense to go beyond the basic coverage. If you are very overweight, a calculation based on your target weight or fat-free mass can make more sense than a rigid calculation based on your current body weight.

In practice, this means: include a clear source of protein per main meal, for example skyr/quark, eggs, fish, meat, tofu/tempeh, legumes or protein powder if necessary.

## 5.3 Eat plant-based

The DGE recommends a diet that consists predominantly of plant-based foods. These include fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds and vegetable oils (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/gut-essen-und-trinken/dge-empfehlungen/">DGE 2024a</a>; <a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/gut-essen-und-trinken/dge-ernaehrungskreis/">DGE 2024b</a>).

A simple grid is sufficient for everyday life: Every main meal consists of a protein source, vegetables or fruit, satiating carbohydrates and a useful source of fat.

## 5.4 Prioritize fruits and vegetables daily

At least 5 servings of fruits and vegetables per day are a good marker of micronutrients, fiber, volume and satiety (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/gut-essen-und-trinken/dge-empfehlungen/">DGE 2024a</a>). A simple start is a portion of fruit for breakfast, vegetables or salad for lunch and dinner, and frozen vegetables as a reserve.

## 5.5 Take fiber seriously

For adults, the guideline value is at least 30 g of fiber per day. Fiber supports satiety, gut health and metabolic health (<a href="https://www.dge.de/wissenschaft/faqs/ausgewaehlte-fragen-und-antworten-zu-ballaststoffen/">DGE o. J.</a>). Good everyday sources include oatmeal, whole grain bread, lentils, beans, chickpeas, potatoes, berries, vegetables, nuts and flax seeds.

## 5.6 Keep drinks simple

The DGE recommends around 1.5 liters of liquid per day, preferably water or unsweetened tea (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/gut-essen-und-trinken/dge-empfehlungen/">DGE 2024a</a>). For athletes, the requirements can be significantly higher due to body size, heat, sweat rate and amount of training; 3-5 liters per day may be plausible on long training or hot days (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/">ACSM 2007</a>).

In everyday life, the rule “drinks are calorie-free” keeps a lot of things simple. Water, mineral water, unsweetened tea and black coffee are the basis; Sugary drinks, juice, smoothies, milkshakes and alcohol are sources of calories and should be planned carefully.

**Zero drinks:** If the real alternative is a sugar drink, zero drinks are clearly the better choice for calorie balance and diet control. Sweeteners are significantly sweeter than sugar and are therefore used in very small quantities; Approved sweeteners provide little or no energy and generally do not increase blood sugar significantly (<a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/high-intensity-sweeteners">FDA 2017</a>). That doesn't mean that zero drinks "make you healthy". They are a tool to replace sugar and liquid calories.

**What happens in the body:** Sweeteners are not a uniform class of substances. Aspartame is rapidly broken down in the intestine into phenylalanine, aspartic acid and methanol; These substances are also found in normal foods. For people with phenylketonuria (PKU), aspartame is relevant because of phenylalanine and must be avoided or strictly limited (<a href="https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/press/news/131210">EFSA 2013</a>; <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/high-intensity-sweeteners">FDA 2017</a>). Other sweeteners such as sucralose, acesulfame-K or steviol glycosides are metabolized differently; What they have in common is that they deliver very high sweetness with little or no energy.

**What is proven:** Randomized studies and systematic reviews suggest that sweeteners can reduce sugar and energy intake in the short term when they replace sugar; The benefit is particularly clear when zero drinks are drunk instead of sugar-sweetened drinks (<a href="https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240046429">WHO 2022</a>; <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790045">McGlynn et al. 2022</a>). It has not been proven that sweeteners trigger fat loss, "destroy" the metabolism or automatically cause cravings regardless of the calorie balance. The WHO does not recommend sweeteners as a standalone long-term strategy for weight control or chronic disease prevention because long-term benefits on body fat are not clearly established and observational data remain uncertain (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK592246/">WHO 2023</a>).

**Pragmatic rule:** Water remains standard. Zero drinks are useful when they replace sugar drinks, juice, iced tea, energy drinks or alcohol calories. They are less useful if they are just additional, trigger a craving for sweets or serve as an excuse to eat more elsewhere.

**Sports context:** For short sessions, water is usually enough. Electrolytes can be useful for long sessions of around 60-90 minutes, high heat, heavy sweating or several sessions per day. Sodium is the most important sweat electrolyte; it supports fluid absorption and fluid retention. Liquid calories in sports drinks are not everyday drinks, but rather targeted energy intake during exercise (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/">ACSM 2007</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4">ISSN 2017b</a>).

**Warning signal:** Don't overdrink compulsively. During long endurance sessions, too much water without enough sodium is problematic because it can increase the risk of hyponatremia (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/">ACSM 2007</a>).

## 5.7 Use carbohydrates as training energy

Carbohydrates are not an antidote to health or fat loss. They are the most important, readily available training fuel for high-volume strength training, intensive sessions, running and repetitive-peak sports (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4">ISSN 2017b</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>).

**Before training:** A meal consisting of carbohydrates plus protein 2-4 hours before training is advisable, e.g. B. Rice/potatoes/oats/bread/pasta plus a protein source. If it's been a long time since your last meal, a small snack 30-60 minutes beforehand can help, e.g. E.g. banana, bread, rice cakes or something easily digestible (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4">ISSN 2017b</a>).

**Post-workout:** Post-workout carbohydrates replenish muscle glycogen. This is particularly important indirectly for building muscle: better glycogen availability supports training performance, training volume, recovery and thus the next effective training stimulus. The main direct stimulus for building muscle protein remains the combination of strength training and sufficient protein (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4">ISSN 2017b</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>).

After training, a normal meal of protein plus carbohydrates is usually enough. This becomes more important if the session was long or very intense, if you train again on the same or the next day or if the goal is to build muscle with a calorie surplus.

There is no mandatory 30-minute window for normal strength training sessions if daily calories and daily protein are appropriate. The higher the training volume, endurance percentage and unit frequency, the more important targeted carb timing becomes (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4">ISSN 2017b</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>).

## 5.8 Improve fat quality

Fats are important for cell functions, the hormonal system and satiety. What is most important is the quality and context of the diet. Canola oil, olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado and fish are good basic sources; Fried snacks, confectionery and highly processed fat-sugar combinations should not be the basis.

## 5.9 Keep alcohol low

There is no risk-free amount of alcohol. If you don't drink, you shouldn't start; Anyone who drinks should keep the amount and frequency low (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/faq/alkohol/">DGE 2024c</a>). Alcohol is not a recovery drink and deserves a critical look, especially during diet, training and sleep phases.

## 5.10 Plan for flexibility

A diet must remain suitable for everyday use. A plan that is too perfect often fails faster than a robust plan with room for maneuver. Favorite foods should be planned for and not treated as a “cheat”; The weekly balance counts more than individual meals.

## 5.11 Nutrition in practice

### 5.11.1 Plate model

- **1/2 plate:** Vegetables, salad or fruit.
- **1/4 plate:** Protein source.
- **1/4 plate:** filling carbohydrates.
- **Addition:** small, high-quality fat source.

### 5.11.2 Plan weight goal separately

If you want to lose fat, you don't need a new nutritional system, but rather a planned application of the basics: moderate deficit, protein, strength training, steps, sleep and trend control. The details are in [06 Diet Phase](06-diaetphase.md).

If you want to build muscles, you don't need any other food habits, just enough energy for training and regeneration. How preservation, controlled bulk phase, classic bulk phase and dirty bulk are to be classified in practice is given in [07 Mass Phase](07-massephase.md).

### 5.11.3 Supplements

Supplements complement, but do not replace, nutrition, training, sleep and consistency. They are particularly useful when there is a specific need, a supply gap or a practical problem. More is not automatically better: when it comes to micronutrients, blood values, dietary patterns, medications, illnesses and safe upper limits count.

**Sensible candidates:**

- **Protein/Whey:** practical when food is not enough (<a href="https://www.dge.de/presse/meldungen/2020/positionspapier-zur-proteinzufuhr-im-sport/">DGE 2020</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>).
- **Creatine Monohydrate:** well studied; often 3-5 g/day as a simple practical dose (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z">ISSN 2017c</a>).
- **Electrolytes/sodium:** Depending on the situation, useful for long, hot or very sweaty endurance exercise (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/">ACSM 2007</a>).
- **Vitamin D:** check in case of little sun, abnormal values ​​or professional recommendation; Do not suspect long-term high dosages. The BfR specifies cautious maximum amounts for dietary supplements and emphasizes that with sufficient sun exposure and a balanced diet, preparations are often not necessary (<a href="https://www.bfr.bund.de/veroeffentlichung/ausgewaehlte-fragen-und-antworten-zu-vitamin-d/">BfR 2025a</a>; <a href="https://www.bfr.bund.de/lebensmittel-und-futtermittelsicherheit/bewertung-der-stofflichen-risiken-von-lebensmitteln/bewertung-von-lebensmittelinhaltsstoffen/bewertung-von-vitaminen-und-mineralstoffen-in-lebensmitteln-neu/">BfR 2025b</a>).
- **Vitamin B12:** practically mandatory for a vegan diet; check if the intake of animal foods is very low. The DGE names vitamin B12 as a critical nutrient and refers to preparations for vegan diets (<a href="https://www.dge.de/wissenschaft/referenzwerte/vitamin-b12/">DGE B12</a>; <a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/faq/faqs-vegane-ernaehrung/">DGE vegan</a>).
- **Iron, Omega-3 and other micronutrients:** Do not take them across the board, but check your diet, blood values, symptoms and target group. This is especially true in cases of severe fatigue, a vegan diet, very low energy intake, heavy menstruation, medications or illnesses.

**Do not prioritize:**

- Detox products.
- Fat burners.
- Testosterone booster without medical evidence.
- expensive special products with no clear benefit.
