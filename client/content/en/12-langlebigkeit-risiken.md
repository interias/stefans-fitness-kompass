This chapter is not a biohacking list. It's about big, robust levers that influence lifespan and healthy lifespan. Individual statistics describe populations, not the fate of a single person. The table in 12.2 is the main overview; the sections after that only expand on points that are otherwise easily misunderstood.

## 12.1 Basic idea: live longer and stay healthy longer

Longevity rarely comes from a single perfect lever, but rather from the sum of a few repeatable risk reductions. The key is not to optimize every factor to the maximum, but rather not to permanently ignore the major construction sites.

In large cohorts, a combination of not smoking, healthy body weight, regular activity, good diet quality, and low/moderate alcohol consumption was associated with significantly longer life expectancy after age 50. In a US evaluation, the magnitude was around 14 years for women and 12 years for men, compared to zero of these low-risk factors (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6207481/">Li et al. 2018</a>). A BMJ review also found more years free of cancer, cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes in people with several healthy lifestyle factors (<a href="https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.l6669">Li et al. 2020</a>). Such numbers are not a guarantee and cannot be transferred 1:1 to every person. But they clearly show that the sum of the basics counts more than the next little health hack.

## 12.3 Classify waist and body weight practically

Body weight alone is not enough. Waist circumference complements the weight trend because belly fat can be more relevant to metabolic and disease risks than a pure number of kilograms.

Measure your waist circumference occasionally under the same conditions and use it as a trend, not as a daily stressor. The measurement method and guideline values ​​are in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md). If weight and waist decrease, strength is maintained as much as possible and everyday life becomes better, this is often a good sign. If you have a significantly increased waist circumference, high blood pressure, risk of diabetes, suspected fatty liver disease or symptoms of sleep apnea, seek medical advice.

## 12.4 Look at cancer risks soberly

Cancer is not completely preventable. Age, genetics, chance, environment and infections play a role. Prevention therefore does not mean controlling every risk, but rather taking the controllable risks from 12.2 seriously and not forgetting special cancer-related topics.

**Cancer-related special topics:** Use UV protection, have vaccinations and infection issues such as HPV/hepatitis checked by a doctor, take advantage of recommended early detection and do not make processed meat the basis of your diet.

Good prevention does not mean fear of every risk. It means soberly reducing known major risks and using professional precautions instead of social media diagnoses when there is uncertainty.

## 12.5 Application: Priorities instead of perfection

1. **Measure what otherwise remains invisible:** Blood pressure, waist circumference, weight trend, preventive care and vaccination status.
2. **Stabilize behavior:** Don't treat exercise, diet, sleep, alcohol limits and quitting smoking as short-term projects.
3. **First close gaps, then optimize:** Small biohacks are of secondary importance as long as large, known risks are permanently open.

What matters for a longer and healthier life is not a perfect biohack, but a system that keeps the major avoidable risks permanently low.
