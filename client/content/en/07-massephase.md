In this compass, mass phase means a planned phase for muscle building, training performance and controlled body weight gain. The goal is not to get heavier as quickly as possible. The goal is to stay in a small surplus long enough for training and regeneration to benefit without gaining unnecessary amounts of body fat.

## 7.1 Maintenance, bulk phase and dirty bulk

The terms do not describe different metabolic laws, but rather different energy surpluses and risk profiles.

| phase | Energy target | Weight trend | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| conservation | approximately maintenance calories | stable 7-day trend | good for technique, getting used to it, recomposition for beginners or after diets |
| Controlled mass phase (Lean Bulk) | small excess, often 100-300 kcal/day | slowly rising | usually the best standard for natural muscle building |
| Classic mass phase (bulk) | larger excess, often 300-500 kcal/day | rising faster | may suit very slim, active or difficult to gain weight individuals, but increases fat gain |
| Dirty Bulk | uncontrolled excess | rising rapidly | usually poor cost-benefit calculation: lots of fat, poorer health markers, longer diet later |

Maintenance calories are not an exact point. They are a range in which weight remains approximately stable over several weeks. If you don't yet know your own conservation value, you should first use the measurement and trend method from [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md) and collect data for 2-3 weeks.

## 7.2 Muscle building has limit growth behavior

Building muscle is limited by training stimulus, training status, genetics, sleep, protein, recovery and time. Eating more can correct low energy levels, but it doesn't make muscle building faster indefinitely. Once there is a sufficient surplus, additional energy increasingly ends up as fat.

The literature is cautious here, but the direction is clear. A review of the role of excess energy emphasizes that building muscle in a deficit can be possible, especially in untrained people or people with higher body fat, but that a conservative excess often makes sense for trained and slim people (<a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131/full">Slater et al. 2019</a>). In a study with elite athletes, higher energy intake led to more body weight and significantly more fat mass, but not more fat-free mass than slower weight gain (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23679146/">Garthe et al. 2013</a>). For natural bodybuilders in the off-season context, a review recommends a more controlled excess and slow weight gain (<a href="https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/325019/nutrition-recommendations-for-bodybuilders-in-the-off-season-a-narrative-review">Iraki et al. 2019</a>).

In practical terms, this means: If weight and waist circumference increase quickly, but training performance, repetitions and set quality hardly improve, the excess is probably larger than necessary.

## 7.3 Start and stop corridor

For male, healthy, training adults, starting a controlled bulking phase at roughly 12-15% body fat is often a useful practice area. This is a bodybuilding and coaching heuristic, not a medical limit. Below this, holding for long periods becomes tiring for many; Above this point, the mass phase becomes shorter because the next cut is needed more quickly. As a pragmatic limit, around 20% body fat is often a good time to end the bulking phase or go into maintenance. These areas do not automatically apply to women, other target groups, other measurement methods or people with medical risks.

Body fat percentages are estimates with significant measurement uncertainty. For health and everyday life, weight trends, waist circumference, blood pressure, blood values, sleep and performance are often more robust. The central measurement methods and warning limits are in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md); the risk classification is in [12 Longevity and Risk Factors](12-langlebigkeit-risiken.md).

The principle is therefore: build up in a controlled manner for as long as possible, but not so long that your waistline, health and later diet costs eat up the benefits.

## 7.4 Select excess

A sensible start is usually $\text{maintenance} + 100\text{-}300\,\text{kcal/day}$ or about 5-10% above conservation. After 2-4 weeks, adjustments are made based on the 7-day weight trend. Natural bodybuilding recommendations often cite around 0.25-0.5% body weight gain per week for beginners and advanced athletes; Advanced athletes should be more conservative because their potential muscle growth will be slower (<a href="https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/325019/nutrition-recommendations-for-bodybuilders-in-the-off-season-a-narrative-review">Iraki et al. 2019</a>).

For 80kg of body weight, 0.25% per week would be around 0.2kg, 0.5% would be around 0.4kg. If you have been training for a long time, you can even be lower. If the trend goes up faster, that is not automatically better. Often it just means that the later cut will be longer.

## 7.5 Macros and food selection

Protein remains secured. For exercisers, the ISSN lists 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day as often a sufficient range for building and maintaining muscle; In the mass phase, more protein is not automatically better if daily protein is already suitable (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>). Carbohydrates are often useful in the bulking phase because they support exercise performance, volume and glycogen availability. Fats remain important, but should not multiply the excess unnoticed.

Controlled bulking doesn't mean just eating "clean" foods. It is said that the base consists of normal, nutrient-dense meals and energy-dense extras are targeted: more rice, potatoes, oats, bread, pasta, fruit, yogurt, olive oil, nuts or an additional snack. Liquid calories, sweets and fast food can provide calories, but often make control worse.

## 7.6 Control and stop signals

A mass phase needs the same measuring points as a diet phase, just in a different direction. The details are in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md): 7-day weight average, waist circumference, training log, sleep, appetite, digestion, blood pressure if necessary and an honest assessment of training progression. A good bulking phase shows slowly increasing weight, stable or only moderately increasing abdominal circumference, and better performance on repeatable exercises.

Stop signals include a rapidly increasing waist circumference, significantly poorer fitness, high blood pressure, poor sleep, frequent feelings of fullness, declining mobility or several weeks of weight gain without training progress. Then maintenance, a small mini-cut or a correction of 100-200 kcal makes more sense than continuing to eat because the “mass phase” is on the plan.

## 7.7 End mass phase

A bulking phase doesn't just end when you feel uncomfortable. It ends when the additional excess creates more diet costs than muscle building benefits. In practical terms, this means: with roughly 20% body fat for men as a heuristic, a sharply increasing waist trend or unfavorable health markers, go for maintenance and decide whether a cut makes sense.

If you start at around 12-15%, increase slowly and stop at around 18-20%, you can often build up productivity for several months. If you start at 20% and gain weight quickly, you usually have to diet again sooner. The longer, controlled bulk phase is therefore usually stronger than the fast dirty bulk with a long repair phase.
