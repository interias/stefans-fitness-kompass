In this compass, tracking means: measuring in order to better understand your own everyday life. It is a tool for learning, not a proof of discipline and not an obligation to permanently grasp everything. Good data makes trends visible, bad data only creates false security. For eating disorders, compulsive controlling behavior, severe fear of weight or calories, binge eating or social withdrawal, less tracking and professional help makes more sense than more accuracy.

## 4.1 Principle: measure, classify, decide

A measurement is only useful if it leads to a better decision. Body weight, calories, macros, training, steps, sleep and body measurements are therefore working values. They show direction and context, but they do not replace body awareness, everyday life, health or medical diagnosis.

Tracking works best as a temporary calibration: record honestly for 2-4 weeks, recognize patterns, change one or two adjustment screws and then simplify again. Tracking can be tighter in the diet phase and bulking phase; In conservation, a few control points are often enough. Systematic reviews on weight management show that self-monitoring can help, especially if nutrition, exercise and weight are recorded regularly and practically (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3268700/">Burke et al. 2011</a>). The benefit doesn't come from the app, but from repeatability and feedback.

## 4.2 Measurement standard: equal conditions instead of more data

More data does not automatically mean better data. What is more important is that measurements are taken under similar conditions and the trend is observed.

| Measured value | Simple means | method | frequency | Border |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body weight | normal scale | in the morning after toilet, before eating and drinking | 3-7x/week, as a 7-day average | Daily weight fluctuates due to salt, carbohydrates, water, digestion and cycle |
| waist | flexible tape measure | same place, band horizontal, stand relaxed, after normal exhalation | 1x/week or every 2 weeks | do not measure daily; small errors are normal |
| Body measurements | Tape measure | Chest, upper arm, hips, thighs only if necessary | every 2-4 weeks | Pump, muscle soreness and measuring point change values |
| Nutrition | Kitchen scales, label, app | Record energy-dense foods, oil, nuts, snacks and standard recipes more accurately | at times, especially in goal phases | App databases and portion sizes are estimates |
| training | Notebook or app | Exercise, load, reps, sets, RIR/RPE, note | each unit | Without technical and contextual notes, progression quickly becomes misleading |
| Blood pressure | Upper arm measuring device | sitting, calm, cuff fitting, multiple measurements | if necessary or at risk | repeatedly clarify high values ​​professionally |
| Steps and sleep | Smartphone, watch, ring | use it as a trend, not as the exact truth | Possible passively every day | Calorie consumption and sleep phases are often inaccurate |

Photos can help if they are taken rarely and in a standardized manner: same time, light, distance, clothing and posture. They are optional. If this causes you to brood more or constantly evaluate yourself, leave it out.

## 4.3 Classify weight, waist and body measurements

Body weight is a noisy measurement. A single day of weighing says little, a 7-day average over several weeks says significantly more. What counts in diet phases is whether the trend is falling; What counts in mass phases is whether the trend increases slowly and whether training and waistline match it.

BMI is a rough population metric: $\text{body weight (kg)} / \text{height (m)}^2$. The CDC classifies 18.5-24.9 as normal weight, 25.0-29.9 as overweight, and over 30 as obese (<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/adult-calculator/bmi-categories.html">CDC 2024</a>). For muscular people, BMI alone is limited because it does not separate fat and muscle mass. That's why waist circumference, blood pressure, blood values, performance and context are more important than a single number.

Waist is often more predictive of health risks than weight alone. NIDDK and NHLBI mention increased risk areas with a waist circumference of more than 102 cm for men and more than 88 cm for women (<a href="https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/adult-overweight-obesity/am-i-healthy-weight">NIDDK 2023</a>; <a href="https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart-healthy-living/healthy-weight">NHLBI o. J.</a>). The waist-to-height ratio is a simple additional metric: $\text{waist circumference} / \text{height}$. NICE recommends keeping your waist below half your height if possible; From around 0.5 the risk increases, from around 0.6 it becomes significantly more problematic (<a href="https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Recommendations">NICE 2025</a>).

Body fat scales, watches and scanners can provide trends, but are not an exact measurement of body fat. Hydration, salt, training, skin temperature and device logic change the display. Anyone who uses such values ​​only evaluates the course over several weeks and not the decimal place.

## 4.4 Track nutrition and macros

Nutrition tracking is intended to calibrate portions. Many people only learn how much energy oil, nuts, cheese, sauces, drinks, snacks and restaurant portions actually provide after 2-4 weeks of weighing. After that, standard portions, fixed meals and occasional samples are often sufficient.

The order is important. The first thing that counts is the energy intake if you want to change your body weight in a targeted manner. Then comes protein, fiber, food choice and whether the plan is filling. Fat and carbohydrates are most often distributed based on preference, training and tolerance, as long as the diet remains nutrient-dense overall.

| Metric | Often a useful target area | Classification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| protein | generally at least reference value; during training often 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day | DGE states 1.2-2.0 g/kg/day for sport, depending on the goal and extent; ISSN lists 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day as a reasonable range (<a href="https://www.dge.de/presse/meldungen/2020/positionspapier-zur-proteinzufuhr-im-sport/">DGE 2020</a>; <a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>) |
| fiber | at least 30 g/day as a guideline | supports satiety and nutritional quality; DGE states 30 g/day for adults (<a href="https://www.dge.de/wissenschaft/faqs/ausgewaehlte-fragen-und-antworten-zu-ballaststoffen/">DGE o. J.</a>) |
| Calories | depending on the goal and trend | Calculators are starting values; Body weight trend decides |
| Fat and carbohydrates | flexible as part of a nutrient-rich diet | Do not optimize in isolation before calories, protein, fiber and food quality are right |

Macros don't have to be perfect every day. A weekly average is usually more meaningful than a single day. Anyone who tracks very precisely should not forget that labels, restaurant information and app databases are only approximations.

## 4.5 Track training

A training log makes progress visible. Minimum is: date, exercise, sets, repetitions, weight and a short note on the set's proximity to failure or technique. For hypertrophy, hard sets per muscle group per week, RIR/RPE, exercise selection and recovery are more important than perfect app evaluation.

```text
Datum:
Schlaf / Energie:
Übung | Gewicht | Wiederholungen | Sätze | RIR/RPE | Notiz
Nächster Schritt:
```

Performance only becomes comparable if the movement remains comparable. More weight with a significantly shorter range of motion, more momentum or poorer control does not automatically mean progress. If there is pain, not only do we continue tracking, but exercise selection, technique, volume and load are also checked.

## 4.6 Formulas and Metrics

Formulas are work values. They help you get started, but the trend decides.

| Metric | formula | To use | Border |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BMI | $\text{kg} / \text{m}^2$ | rough weight category | does not separate muscle and fat mass |
| Waist to height ratio | $\text{waist circumference} / \text{height}$ | simple risk classification for central fat distribution | The measuring point and body type influence the value |
| 7 day weight cut | $\frac{\text{sum of the last 7 morning weights}}{7}$ | smoothes water and digestive fluctuations | needs regular measurement |
| Weight change per week | $\frac{\text{trend week 2} - \text{trend week 1}}{\text{body weight}} \times 100$ | Control diet or mass phase | too loud for short periods of time |
| Mifflin-St.-Jeor Men | $10 \times \text{kg} + 6{,}25 \times \text{cm} - 5 \times \text{age} + 5$ | Starting value for resting energy consumption | Activity factor and adjustment remain necessary (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/">Mifflin et al. 1990</a>) |
| Mifflin-St.-Jeor Women | $10 \times \text{kg} + 6{,}25 \times \text{cm} - 5 \times \text{age} - 161$ | Starting value for resting energy consumption | individual consumption may vary |
| Preservation from trend | $\text{average calories} - \frac{\text{weight change kg/week} \times 7{.}700}{7}$ | roughly calculate personal preservation | Water, salt, cycle, disease and tracking errors distort |
| Deficit or surplus | $\text{target calories} - \text{maintenance calories}$ | Plan energy goal | small differences get lost in the noise of everyday life |
| e1RM to Epley | $\text{weight} \times (1 + \frac{\text{repetitions}}{30})$ | Roughly estimate the force trend | only usable with similar technique and moderate repetitions |

1 kg of body fat roughly corresponds to 7,700 kcal. This is a calculation anchor, not a promise from the scales. Dynamic models such as the NIDDK Body Weight Planner show more precisely that body weight, energy expenditure and adaptations influence each other (<a href="https://www.niddk.nih.gov/bwp">NIDDK Body Weight Planner</a>; <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880593/">Hall et al. 2011</a>).

## 4.7 Guide values ​​and warning limits

Guideline values ​​are guidance, not a diagnosis. A single number only becomes important if it is repeatedly noticeable or occurs together with complaints, medications, illnesses or severe suffering.

| Area | Often a useful target or test value | Warning signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diet phase | around 0.5-1.0% body weight per week | permanent rapid loss, loss of performance, sleep problems, binge eating |
| Mass phase | around 0.25-0.5% body weight per week | Waist rises quickly, performance does not increase, blood pressure or sleep worsens |
| waist | under 102 cm men, under 88 cm women; Additionally, aim for a waist-to-height ratio of less than 0.5 | significantly higher, rising rapidly or together with blood pressure/blood sugar problems |
| Blood pressure | below 140/90 mmHg as a rough guide; below 120/80 mmHg is a very favorable range | repeatedly from 140/90 mmHg, clarify significantly fluctuating values, symptoms or individual risks; From around 130/80 mmHg, lifestyle and follow-up monitoring is often worthwhile (<a href="https://gesund.bund.de/bluthochdruck">gesund.bund.de o. J.</a>; <a href="https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/nvl-009k_S3_Hypertonie_2023-06.pdf">AWMF/NVL 2023</a>; <a href="https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings">AHA 2024</a>) |
| Movement | at least 150 minutes moderate or 75 minutes intensive per week plus 2 days of strength training | permanently very little movement or a sharp drop in resilience (<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html">CDC 2025c</a>) |
| Sleep | Adults often need at least 7 hours | permanently less than 6 hours, severe daytime tiredness, snoring with pauses in breathing (<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html">CDC 2024b</a>) |
| Tracking behavior | Data makes decisions easier | Fear, compulsion, social withdrawal, constant recalculation or compensation |

## 4.8 Tools, Apps and Notebook

Apps can save work, especially with trends and databases. But they can also create friction, distraction and false precision. For many, one app per category or a simple notebook is enough.

| category | Examples | What to use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight trend | <a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.cachapa.libra">Libra</a>, <a href="https://happyscale.com/">Happy Scale</a>, <a href="https://github.com/oliexdev/openScale">openScale</a> | Smooth out daily weight, see trends, classify plateaus more soberly |
| Nutrition | <a href="https://fddb.info/">FDDB</a>, <a href="https://www.yazio.com/">YAZIO</a>, <a href="https://www.myfitnesspal.com/">MyFitnessPal</a> | Roughly control calories, protein, fiber, meals and recipes |
| training | <a href="https://www.hevyapp.com/">Hevy</a>, <a href="https://www.strong.app/">Strong</a>, <a href="https://www.fitnotesapp.com/">FitNotes</a> | Document exercises, load, reps, sets, RIR/RPE, rest and progression |
| Wearables | Watch, ring, pedometer | Monitor steps, resting heart rate, sleep duration and stress trends |

A classic notebook has real advantages: it's fast, offline, less distracting and allows context instead of just numbers. “Slept poorly, squats are difficult, technique is stable” is often more valuable than ten automatically calculated scores. A notebook also forces you to choose: What is really relevant to the decision?

## 4.9 Minimal Setups

A simple setup is often enough for maintenance: log training, check weight trends occasionally, keep an eye on steps or movement and reflect briefly every few weeks.

For a diet phase, more narrow measurements are taken: 7-day weight average, calories or standard portions, protein, steps, exercise, hunger, sleep and weekly waist trend. Details about the implementation can be found in [06 Diet Phase](06-diaetphase.md).

What counts for a mass phase are weight trends, waist circumference, training progression, sleep, digestion and appetite. Calories are only tracked as precisely as necessary to keep the excess small. The practical control is in [07 Mass Phase](07-massephase.md).
