Diet phase in this compass means a planned fat loss phase, not a nutritional identity. The goal is a moderate deficit that remains repeatable long enough without unnecessarily damaging training, sleep, daily routine, and nutrient coverage. In the case of eating disorders, excessive weight, pregnancy, medication, illnesses, noticeable fatigue or very aggressive diets, professional support is useful.

## 6.1 Understanding energy balance

Body weight changes when energy intake and energy expenditure do not match over time. This is not a daily account, but a dynamic system: less body weight reduces energy expenditure, activity fluctuates, water and glycogen mask fat loss in the short term, and people often underestimate calories. That's why calculators are just starting values; the trend decides (<a href="https://www.niddk.nih.gov/bwp">NIDDK Body Weight Planner</a>; <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880593/">Hall et al. 2011</a>).

The principle remains simple: fat loss requires a repeatable deficit. Food quality, protein, fiber, solid meals and a suitable environment are not an alternative to energy balance, but rather make it practical.

## 6.2 Use maintenance calories as work value

A calorie goal is first a work value. Maintenance calories can be estimated using calculators or derived from 2-3 weeks of weight trends and nutrition tracking. The formulas, measuring rules and limits are collected in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md). The only practical question that counts for the diet is: Does the trend fall within the planned corridor without training, sleep and everyday life suffering unnecessarily?

## 6.3 Planning a deficit

A deficit is deducted from the conservation value. 200 kcal/day is conservative and performance-preserving, but slow. 300-500 kcal/day is a good starting range for many if protein, sleep and training fit. 1,000 kcal/day is not a normal recommendation, but a hard special phase with clear limits: hunger, training performance, everyday energy, regeneration, nutrient coverage and muscle maintenance become problems more quickly.

| Daily deficit | Weekly deficit | coarse fat equivalent |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 200 kcal | 1,400 kcal | approx. 0.18 kg/week |
| 300 kcal | 2,100 kcal | approx. 0.27 kg/week |
| 500 kcal | 3,500 kcal | approx. 0.45 kg/week |
| 1,000 kcal | 7,000 kcal | approx. 0.9 kg/week |

The calorie values ​​are calculation anchors, not scale promises. For many diet phases, a loss of around 0.5-1.0% of body weight per week is a realistic range; Official weight management sources emphasize slow, steady change, and exercise-related recommendations are particularly cautious with slim, exercising individuals (<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/losing-weight/index.html">CDC 2025</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24864135/">Helms et al. 2014</a>). How this corridor is measured is shown in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md).

A deficit of 1,000 kcal/day is a short, special phase, not a standard solution. Often a sensible limit: 4-6 weeks, then 1-2 weeks of maintenance or a switch to a more moderate deficit. This refers to the deficit, not a total intake of 1,000 kcal/day. Such an approach without professional support is not suitable for people of normal weight or very slim, at risk of eating disorders, pregnancy, medication, illnesses, noticeable fatigue, RED-S warning signals or very high training load. Very low total intakes belong in a different context: NICE classifies 800-1,200 kcal/day and below 800 kcal/day as low or very low energy diets and only recommends them with nutritional completeness, professional support, clear indications and limited duration (<a href="https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Physical-activity-and-diet">NICE 2025b</a>).

## 6.4 Hunger, satiety and hormonal adjustment

Hunger in the diet is not just a matter of the head. Leptin, ghrelin, PYY, GLP-1, CCK and other signals link energy availability, digestion, body fat, brain and appetite. After weight loss, reviews often show a pattern that makes further weight loss more difficult: Ghrelin, as a hunger signal, tends to increase, while several satiety signals tend to fall or react more weakly. The findings are not equally stable for every hormone, but the direction is practically relevant: the longer and the harder a diet goes, the more important satiety, sleep, training control and breaks become (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28934819/">Zhao et al. 2017</a>; <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41366-025-01726-4">Jin et al. 2025</a>).

This adaptation does not mean that the metabolism is broken. Rather, it explains why a deficit creates more friction over time: more thoughts about food, less spontaneous movement, poorer desire to exercise, stronger reaction to very easy-to-eat foods. In practice, there is therefore no attempt to “optimize” individual hormones. The robust levers are tested: enough protein, high-fiber and voluminous meals, sufficient carbohydrates around hard units, sleep, planned meals, fewer trigger foods in direct access and a deficit that can still be repeated.

## 6.5 When the cut tips over: Warning signals instead of hormonal panic

“Hormonal crash” is not a clean diagnostic term. It can still be useful as a warning word if it indicates a period of low energy availability that is too hard or too long. It becomes particularly problematic when deficits, a lot of training, little sleep, low body fat percentage and high everyday stress come together.

Warning signals include permanently declining training performance, unusually severe tiredness, feeling cold, sleep problems, irritability, loss of libido, cycle changes or missing periods, repeated infections, susceptibility to injury, binge eating, social withdrawal or compulsive controlling behavior. The IOC describes Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport as a result of problematic, prolonged or severe low energy availability with possible effects on energy expenditure, reproduction, bone, immune system, glycogen synthesis and cardiovascular and blood health (<a href="https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073">IOC 2023</a>). With such signals, the first step is no longer discipline, but rather reducing the deficit, planning maintenance, checking the training load and seeking professional advice if there are significant symptoms.

## 6.6 Refeed and Diet Breaks

A refeed is a planned short step towards maintenance calories, usually for 1-2 days and primarily via more carbohydrates. It's not an unplanned cheat weekend or a metabolic reset. It can be useful if you want hard training sessions to go better, glycogen and water levels increase slightly, the diet becomes more psychologically acceptable or a social event needs to be properly planned. The weekly balance remains crucial.

In exercisers, Campbell et al. a 7-week deficit with 5 restrictive days plus 2 higher carbohydrate refeed days on maintenance and found evidence of better maintenance of lean mass and resting energy expenditure than with continuous deficit; the study was small and is not a free pass for arbitrary refeed rules (<a href="https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5142/5/1/19">Campbell et al. 2020</a>). In practice, it's often enough: leave the protein the same, don't increase the fat unnecessarily, increase carbohydrates, set calories to approximately maintenance and then continue eating normally.

A diet break is longer: usually 7-14 days on maintenance calories. It is more suitable after several hard weeks of dieting, when you are very tired of dieting or after a 4-6 week aggressive phase. In the MATADOR study in men with obesity, switching between deficit and maintenance blocks resulted in more fat loss than continuous restriction, but the protocol took significantly longer and is not automatically transferable to any regular diet (<a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/ijo2017206">Byrne et al. 2018</a>). A sober weekly check is often more useful than a perfect protocol: Is the deficit still tolerable, or does it need maintenance before the diet ends?

## 6.7 Classify abbreviations and diet hacks soberly

Diet hacks are not a shortcut around energy balance. Only levers that make the deficit possible with less friction make sense.

Protein per main meal is the first lever because it supports satiety and muscle maintenance. Vegetables, fruits, potatoes, legumes, soups and salads help with volume, water and fiber; the CDC describes exactly this volume logic as a practical weight management strategy (<a href="https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/healthy-eating/fruits-vegetables.html">CDC 2025b</a>). Calorie-free drinks, zero drinks as a replacement for sugary drinks, solid standard meals and prepared emergency options reduce the burden of decision-making.

The strongest “hack” is often the environment. Heavily processed, very easy-to-eat foods are not automatically banned, but they increase spontaneous calorie intake for many. In a controlled NIH study, participants consumed significantly more energy and gained weight on ultra-processed diet ad libitum, even though the diets offered were tailored to several nutritional values ​​(<a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008">Hall et al. 2019</a>). In practical terms, this means: Don't store trigger foods in bulk at home, but instead portion them in specific portions or eat them away from home.

## 6.8 Classify fasting and intermittent fasting soberly

In this compass, fasting is not a separate mechanism of action, but rather one of several ways to structure a deficit. Intermittent fasting (e.g. 16:8 or 5:2) and time slot eating primarily shift *when* you eat, not necessarily *how much*. Fat loss still occurs through energy balance, not through time.

In calorie-adjusted comparisons, intermittent fasting is not superior to continuous deficit. In a 12-month study, an 8-hour eating window plus a deficit resulted in similar weight loss to a deficit alone (8.0 vs. 6.3 kg, no significant difference) (<a href="https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833">Liu et al. 2022</a>). Time window eating can support fat loss, but works primarily because the narrow window lowers total calories and provides no additional benefit for blood pressure, blood sugar or blood lipids compared to a normal deficit (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10630127/">Sun et al. 2023</a>).

In practical terms, this means that fasting is a good tool if it makes it easier to implement. For some, a late first meal or early last meal is the easiest way to cut back on evening snacks, alcohol or unplanned calories and hit the deficit without constant counting. For others, a narrow eating window leads to cravings, poorer workouts, or compensatory overeating. The DGE therefore assesses intermittent fasting cautiously: possible advantages, including a tendency for less muscle loss, but a lack of long-term studies and, above all, no guidelines for food selection. Fasting is not a substitute for good nutrition in the eating window (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/diaeten-und-fasten/intervallfasten/">DGE Intervallfasten</a>).

Water fasting and multi-day zero diet fasting are different from intermittent fasting and are not recommended as diet aids for healthy adults. Longer periods of pure fasting increase muscle loss and loss of performance; When returning to work, there is a risk of unfavorable metabolic shifts and even refeeding syndrome with dangerous electrolyte and fluid changes. Even in an accompanied study with water fasting for several days, insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) doubled after diet reconstruction (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8951503/">Scharf et al. 2022</a>). Such phases, if at all, belong in a medically supervised context with clear indications, not in a self-controlled diet. The warning signals from [6.5](#65-wenn-der-cut-kippt-warnsignale-statt-hormonpanik) and the limits of very low total intakes from [6.3](#63-defizit-planen) apply more strictly here.

Fasting is unsuitable if you have eating disorders or are at risk, if you have diabetes or are taking blood sugar-lowering medication, if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or if you have a very high training load or a low body fat percentage (RED-S, → [6.5](#65-wenn-der-cut-kippt-warnsignale-statt-hormonpanik)). Here, professional clarification is more important than a specific eating rhythm.

## 6.9 Hidden calories and snack traps

In a diet, the deficit often fails not because of the planned main meal, but because of small amounts that are not perceived as a meal. Oil, nuts, cheese, dressing, sauces, spreads, tasting bites while cooking, leftovers from the plate, the bar in the car and the coffee with milk still count towards the energy balance.

Oil spray is a good example. It can help to dose fat better, but is not calorie-free if sprayed for several seconds or the pan is visibly oiled. Nutritional information refers to the serving shown; If you use two or more portions, you have to think about the calories accordingly (<a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/calories-nutrition-facts-label">FDA o. J.</a>). For US labels, very small amounts of energy under 5 kcal per serving may also be rounded as 0 kcal (<a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-A/section-101.9">21 CFR 101.9</a>). In practice, this means: weigh the oil, nut butter, dressing and mayo in the diet at least temporarily or dose it with teaspoons and tablespoons. One tablespoon of oil can provide approximately 100-120 kcal; Two free shots of oil can noticeably reduce a planned daily deficit.

Snack traps usually arise from availability plus a lack of boundaries. Chips from the bag, nuts next to the laptop, sweets in the kitchen, cheese cubes while cooking, protein bars "for safety" or little things at work are individually small and relevant overall. The solution is not prohibition, but visible portion: snack on a plate, pack back, set snack time or consciously plan snack as part of the meal.

Drinks remain a separate test point in the diet. Juice, smoothies, lattes, cocoa, energy drinks, alcohol and several coffees with milk or syrup can replace a meal, but are often less filling. When fat loss stalls, four places are checked first: cooking fat and dressings, liquid calories, unplanned snacks and weekend amounts. Only when these points are plausible is it worth further reducing calories.

## 6.10 Diet-friendly meals and shopping list

A diet-friendly meal usually needs four building blocks: a clear source of protein, lots of vegetables or fruit, satiating carbohydrates in a controlled amount and a source of fat that doesn't escalate unnoticed. This is not a special meal, but a normal diet with better dosage.

| building block | Shopping list | Simple meals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| protein | Skyr, low-fat quark, eggs, chicken, turkey breast, tuna, salmon, lean mince, tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans | Skyr with berries, omelet with vegetables, chili with beans, tofu and vegetable stir-fry |
| Volume and fiber | Frozen vegetables, lettuce, cucumber, tomatoes, peppers, broccoli, berries, apples, legumes | large bowl, soup, salad with protein, vegetable stir-fry |
| Filling carbohydrates | Potatoes, oatmeal, rice, wholemeal bread, wholemeal pasta, wraps, couscous | Potato and quark plate, oat skyr bowl, rice and vegetable pan |
| fat and taste | Olive oil, rapeseed oil, nuts, avocado, yogurt dip, mustard, salsa, spices, herbs | Measure out the dressing, yogurt-herb sauce, spicy tomato sauce |
| Emergency options | Protein pudding, cottage cheese, prepared potatoes, canned beans, frozen berries, chopped vegetables | 5-minute quark, quick bean pan, bread with protein topping |

The list is intentionally simple. It's intended to make shopping and repetition easier, not to optimize every meal. Those who regularly eat the same 5-8 meals of acceptable quality often have more control than someone who looks for a new, perfect diet recipe every day.

## 6.11 Control and Adjustment

A diet is controlled by trends: 7-day weight cut, waist size, workouts, steps, hunger, sleep and mood. The measurement standard is [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md). Stagnation is only assessed after 2-3 weeks of trend, not after a salty dinner. If weight and waist do not fall, first check tracking errors, weekend, drinks, snacks and portion sizes. Then either lower 100-200 kcal or increase steps slightly.

Warning signals are not tracked away. When sleep, hunger, mood, performance and everyday life deteriorate over several weeks, the diet is no longer just a math problem. Deficit, training load, steps, refeed or diet break and, if necessary, specialist help are then checked.
