Most plans fail not because of a lack of knowledge, but because of friction in everyday life. Good intentions therefore require small, repeatable systems: clear triggers, simple minimum versions, a suitable environment and a weekly check that corrects rather than evaluates.

## 3.1 Habits and 1% method

The 1% method according to James Clear is a rule of thought: small, repeated improvements have a greater long-term effect than rare, radical restarts. It is not an exact mathematical health formula, but a practical model for consistency (<a href="https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/543993/atomic-habits-by-james-clear/9780735211292/">Clear 2018</a>; German edition: <a href="https://www.penguin.de/buecher/james-clear-die-1-methode-minimale-veraenderung-maximale-wirkung/paperback/9783442178582">Clear dt.</a>).

Habits are formed through repetition in a stable context. What is important is not a magical daily number, but rather that a behavior is regularly linked to a clear trigger (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674">Lally et al. 2010</a>; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1">Gollwitzer &amp; Sheeran 2006</a>).

### 3.1.1 Practice rules

1. **Define the smallest version:** The habit must be feasible even on bad days.
2. **Link to trigger:** "After X, I'll do Y" is better than "I should do Y more often."
3. **Lower Friction:** Have workout clothes ready, plan standard meals, place water where you can see.
4. **Increase friction for bad options:** Store candy out of sight, put your cell phone out of the bedroom in the evening.
5. **Strengthen Identity:** Not "I'm trying to exercise" but "I'm someone who does my minimum version."
6. **Plan for relapses:** A missed day is not a cancellation. Missing two days in a row is a warning sign.
7. **Make progress visible:** Checkmarks, training logs, step trends or weekly notes are enough.

### 3.1.2 Examples

- **Nutrition:** Fill a water bottle after breakfast.
- **Training:** Put on your training clothes straight after work.
- **Exercise:** Walk or mobilize for 2 minutes after every long period of sitting.
- **Sleep:** After brushing your teeth, charge your phone outside the bedroom.
- **Diet:** If you feel hungry in the evening, eat the protein/vegetable option first and then decide again.

### 3.1.3 Minimum Versions

- 10 minutes of strength training instead of no training at all.
- 15 minute walk instead of a complete lack of exercise.
- A protein-rich meal instead of perfect nutrition.
- Lights off and cell phone away instead of a perfect evening routine.

Habits should start so small that they start reliably and should be planned so clearly that they do not have to be renegotiated every day.

## 3.2 Everyday life and systems

### 3.2.1 Basic rules

1. Set a training slot, don't hope spontaneously.
2. Define standard meals that always work.
3. Simplify shopping: protein, vegetables, fruits, carbohydrates, controllable snacks.
4. Design the environment: What is visible is more likely to be eaten or done.
5. Have a fallback plan: a bad week means a minimal week, not a break.
6. Weekly trend instead of daily judgment.
7. Document progress: a few key figures are enough; Measurement methods are available in [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md).
8. Reward doesn't just come from food: sauna, walk, equipment, free time, sleep.
9. Filter social media: no sources of fear, hype or detox.
10. Conduct a monthly review: What works? What's annoying? What is too complicated?

### 3.2.2 Decision rules

**Weight stagnates for 2-3 weeks:** first check measurement standard and trend, then calorie intake, protein/fiber/saturation, steps or a small calorie adjustment.

**Training stagnates:** Check sleep, technique, exercise selection, set proximity to failure, volume and deload.

**Motivation low:** Reduce the plan, don't cancel it. Two sessions per week is a sensible minimum standard.

**Hunger High in Diet:** Check protein, vegetables, potatoes/oats/whole grains, sleep, drink calories, and snack environment.

**Constantly tired:** Check sleep, stress, calories, iron/vitamin D/B12 if needed, training volume and alcohol.

## 3.3 Weekly checklist

```text
[ ] 2-4 Krafttrainings erledigt
[ ] 2+ Ausdauer- oder Bewegungseinheiten erledigt
[ ] an den meisten Tagen Protein getroffen
[ ] an den meisten Tagen Obst/Gemüse hoch gehalten
[ ] Ballaststoffquellen gegessen
[ ] 1-2 Mahlzeiten vorbereitet oder geplant
[ ] Schlafroutine an den meisten Tagen eingehalten
[ ] Alkohol niedrig gehalten oder vermieden
[ ] Gewichtstrend geprüft, Tagesgewicht nicht überbewertet
[ ] nächste Trainingswoche geplant
```
