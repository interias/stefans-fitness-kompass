Endurance training doesn't have to follow a competition plan. Even a calm, repeatable mix of loose basic work, slowly increasing volume and a few hard stimuli can improve cardiovascular fitness, oxygen utilization and resilience. Steps remain the basis of everyday life; Endurance training is the planned stimulus.

## 10.1 Supplement stamina

Endurance training improves cardiovascular fitness, resilience, recovery and everyday life. It doesn't have to be hard every time. For adults, the WHO guidelines list 150-300 minutes of moderate or 75-150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity per week as a robust health baseline, in addition to strength training (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/">WHO 2020</a>).

**Why it matters:** Regular aerobic activity is associated with lower all-cause mortality, lower cardiovascular mortality, lower risk of hypertension, type 2 diabetes and certain cancers, and better outcomes in anxiety and depression symptoms, cognitive health and sleep (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/">WHO 2020</a>). Cardiorespiratory fitness is also a strong marker of health; in large cohorts, higher fitness is consistently associated with lower risk of mortality and cardiovascular events (<a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1108396">Kodama et al. 2009</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27881567/">Ross et al. 2016</a>).

In practice this means: lots of easy sessions, few hard sessions and slowly increasing the amount of running. If you don't have much time, you can start with 2 easy sessions per week and add a targeted interval session later.

**Distinction from steps:** Everyday movement and steps are the basis, cardio is the targeted training stimulus. If you want to lose fat, you should first establish a stable pattern of exercise and then decide whether additional cardio is necessary for fitness, health or calorie consumption. If you want to run, improve OCR or endurance performance, you need structured endurance training; Steps alone are not enough.

## 10.2 What cardio improves in the body

Cardio isn't just about burning calories. It trains the ability to absorb and transport oxygen and use it for energy in the muscles.

Mitochondria are a good example of the peripheral side of endurance training. They are not foreign bodies, but firmly integrated cell organelles; In evolutionary terms, however, they have a bacterial history: they have their own, mostly circular DNA and are considered to be descendants of bacterial symbionts that lived permanently in larger cells. Today, most mitochondrial proteins are still encoded and imported by the cell nucleus (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK9896/">Cooper 2000</a>). The trend is practically relevant: repeated endurance stimuli can improve the mitochondrial makeup of the muscles and local oxygen utilization, not as a single biohack, but as a normal adaptation to regularly repeated stress (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787188/">Mølmen et al. 2025</a>).

**Adjustments:** Regular endurance training can improve central factors such as stroke volume, blood volume and cardiovascular performance, as well as peripheral factors such as mitochondria, capillarization and local oxygen utilization (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/">Helgerud et al. 2007</a>; <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787188/">Mølmen et al. 2025</a>).

For your health and everyday life, it is not important to make every session as hard as possible. What is crucial is a repeatable mix of easy endurance, sufficient total volume and occasionally harder stimuli if regeneration, joints and sleep support it.

## 10.3 Understanding VO2max

VO2max describes the maximum oxygen uptake: how much oxygen the body can absorb, transport and utilize during hard exercise. It is often given as ml/kg/min relative to body weight.

VO2max is not a perfect single value or a complete health diagnosis. However, it is a good marker for cardiorespiratory fitness. Watches and apps only estimate VO2 max indirectly; Real value comes from performance diagnostics with breathing gas analysis or well-standardized tests.

**How ​​to increase VO2max:** VO2max responds to regular endurance training. Easy to moderate sessions build volume with low regeneration costs; hard intervals provide stronger stimuli at the upper limit of performance. Greater improvements are often seen with a lower starting level, higher training frequency and consistent increase in load over several weeks (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787188/">Mølmen et al. 2025</a>; <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1440244018309198">Wen et al. 2019</a>).

Only after 6-8 weeks of regular training is it worth evaluating individual VO2max estimates. Trends are better than daily values: same distance with a lower heart rate, higher pace with similar effort, better recovery and more stable interval performance.

## 10.4 Zone 2

"Zone 2" is used differently depending on the system. In this document, Zone 2 means easy to moderate endurance below the first lactate or ventilation threshold: breathing is increased but controlled, and short sets are still possible.

Zone 2 is useful because it allows a lot of training time with a relatively low recovery cost. But it is not a magical special stimulus. For VO2max, performance and time economy, higher intensities can also make sense as long as they remain dosed (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8493117/">MacIntosh et al. 2021</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560504/">Storoschuk et al. 2025</a>). Practically, 30-60 minutes of brisk walking, easy jogging, cycling, rowing machines, cross trainers or swimming are suitable. The talk test is often more useful than rigid pulse zones: If talking is hardly possible, it is usually too hard for zone 2.

## 10.5 HIIT

HIIT means high-intensity interval training: hard work intervals alternate with easy recovery phases. It is a tool for VO2 max, performance and time efficiency, not a mandatory program.

HIIT can effectively improve VO2max. Reviews show that different forms of HIIT work; longer intervals starting at about 2 minutes, at least about 15 minutes of hard total work, and programs lasting 4-12 weeks are particularly strong when VO2max maximization is the goal (<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1440244018309198">Wen et al. 2019</a>).

The limit is fatigue. If you're new, don't sleep well, are in pain, are very stressed or are planning hard leg training days, you should dose carefully. If you have cardiovascular disease, chest pain, dizziness, unexplained shortness of breath or severely elevated blood pressure, seek medical advice before hard intervals.

First warm up, then work hard but in a controlled manner, then coast down or drive out. For many, 1 HIIT session per week is enough in addition to easy cardio. Do not do HIIT immediately before heavy leg or running loads if performance or joints suffer.

## 10.6 Three robust HIIT templates

These protocols are good tools, but not mandatory. The best option is the one that can be repeated regularly, cleanly and without unnecessary discomfort.

| protocol | Implementation | Fits well if | Border |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4x4** | 10 minutes easy, then 4x4 minutes hard at around 85-95% HRmax or RPE 8-9, 3 minutes easy in between, then slow down or extended for 5-10 minutes | VO2max should be specifically improved and longer hard intervals should work well mentally | very demanding; not necessary as an introduction without a stamina base (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/">Helgerud et al. 2007</a>) |
| **10x1** | 8-12x60 seconds hard, 60-75 seconds easy in between | There is little time or 4-minute intervals are still too long | drive or run hard enough, but not as a sprint competition (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20100740/">Little et al. 2010</a>) |
| **20/10 Tabata/SIT** | 8x20 seconds very hard, with 10 seconds of rest in between | very short, very intense stimuli are desired on a bike, rowing machine or ergometer | extremely hard in the original; Often unnecessarily risky for beginners and high running loads (<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8897392/">Tabata et al. 1996</a>) |
