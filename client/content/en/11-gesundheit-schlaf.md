Health is the part of the plan that rarely sounds loud, but supports almost everything. Sleep, everyday exercise, stress, pain and basic medical checks often determine whether training and nutrition remain stable or whether they keep falling out of sync.

## 11.1 Treat sleep as the main lever

Lack of sleep impairs physical and mental performance, psyche, hunger regulation and regeneration (<a href="https://gesund.bund.de/gut-schlafen">gesund.bund.de 2023</a>). Healthy adults should regularly sleep 7 or more hours (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434546/">AASM/SRS 2015</a>); individual needs vary. What matters is whether you are relaxed and productive during the day (<a href="https://gesund.bund.de/gut-schlafen">gesund.bund.de 2023</a>).

## 11.2 Sleep routine before sleep hacks

Regular times, a dark room, a cool environment, an evening routine and less late-night caffeine often bring more benefits than individual hacks. A useful evening routine lowers the stimulation level 60 minutes before bed: check light, work, food, alcohol and doomscrolling.

## 11.3 Clarify chronic sleep problems

If you have persistent problems falling asleep or staying asleep that interfere with daytime sleep, you may have insomnia. The AWMF/DGSM guidelines name cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia as the first treatment option (<a href="https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/063-003l_S3_Insomnie-bei-Erwachsenen_2025-04.pdf">AWMF/DGSM 2025</a>). Don't just experiment with melatonin, alcohol or sleeping pills for months.

## 11.4 Collect everyday movement and steps

It's not just training that counts. Everyday exercise is its own lever for health, energy consumption and diet control. Sitting for long periods of time should be interrupted regularly (<a href="https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/5_Publikationen/Praevention/Broschueren/Konsenspapier_Runder_Tisch.pdf">BMG o. J.</a>; <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/">WHO 2020</a>).

Steps are low-threshold, easy to dose and usually generate little regeneration costs compared to additional hard training. In large cohort data, higher daily step counts are associated with lower all-cause mortality; the benefit does not increase linearly indefinitely, but levels off in higher ranges (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9289978/">Paluch et al. 2022</a>). This doesn't result in a magical 10,000-step law, but it does provide a clear direction: for many people, more regular exercise is better than sitting a lot.

During diet phases, steps can help to create a moderate energy deficit without directly reducing the amount of food. This is particularly useful when hunger, fatigue or dieting stress is already high. Steps do not replace a calorie balance, but they often make them easier to control: 2,000 additional steps per day are not a huge lever, but they are a stable, everyday contribution for weeks.

Steps are everyday exercise, cardio is targeted endurance training. Both make sense, but are not identical. Steps are ideal for reducing sitting and gently increasing daily energy expenditure. Cardio provides greater stimulus for cardiovascular fitness, VO2 max, running performance or athletic goals, but depending on the intensity, it can produce more fatigue and planning effort. Appetitive responses to exercise are individual; On average, studies show no simple rule that training automatically triggers more eating (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8365695/">Beaulieu et al. 2021</a>).

First measure the normal cut for 7 days, then increase it in small steps, e.g. B. around 1,000-2,000 steps per day. Good anchors are phone calls while walking, 10 minutes after meals, short walks, stairs, parking further away, getting off one stop earlier or 2-5 minutes of exercise per hour. During diet phases, a stable step range is often more helpful than individual heroic cardio sessions.

## 11.5 Count stress as a burden

Stress affects sleep, hunger regulation, regeneration and training quality. It should therefore not only be viewed mentally, but as a real stress factor in the training plan.

## 11.6 Prioritize medical base

Prevention, dental status, blood pressure, relevant blood values ​​and vaccination status are less spectacular than supplements, but more important. In the event of fatigue, loss of performance, pain or symptoms, not only optimize training and nutrition, but also check medical causes.

## 11.7 Mental health and exercise

Exercise is a robust, well-documented factor in mental health. A network meta-analysis of more than 200 randomized trials shows that aerobic exercise, walking, strength training, and yoga reduce symptoms of mild to moderate depression with effect sizes comparable to psychotherapy or antidepressants (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11131084/">Noetel et al. 2024</a>). The AWMF S3 guideline for unipolar depression explicitly recommends aerobic training and strength training as a supplementary treatment option (<a href="https://register.awmf.org/de/leitlinien/detail/nvl-005">AWMF NVL-005</a>). For severe or chronic depression, sport alone is not sufficient and does not replace psychotherapy or medical treatment.

Lack of exercise is not only the opposite of training, but also a risk factor in its own right: Sitting for long hours every day is associated with an increased risk of depression and anxiety in dose-response analyzes (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/">WHO 2020</a>). This complements what is stated in [11.4](#114-alltagsbewegung-und-schritte-sammeln) and [10 Endurance Training](10-ausdauertraining.md) about everyday exercise: less sitting and regular exercise have an impact on mental health even below a training threshold.

The connection is particularly relevant for women in perimenopause and the years thereafter. The transition to menopause is biologically associated with an increased risk of depressive episodes, irritability and anxiety — not only in response to body changes, but because hormonal fluctuations affect the serotonergic system and sleep regulation (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9889489/">Shobeiri et al. 2023</a>). A meta-analysis of 21 randomized trials shows that regular exercise measurably reduces symptoms of depression and anxiety in this phase of life; In addition to aerobic units, mind-body approaches such as yoga, Tai Chi and Pilates show particularly good evidence in this group (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11762881/">Hu et al. 2025</a>).

Overtraining can reverse the picture: If you keep the load too high for weeks and don't regenerate enough, you can develop low moods, irritability, sleep disorders and a lack of drive, which are neurobiologically related to states of exhaustion. If performance, sleep and mood decline simultaneously over several weeks, this is a warning sign - training load and recovery should be assessed first before increasing intensity or volume.

If low mood, exhaustion, loss of drive or anxiety symptoms persist for more than two weeks, professional advice is more important than any training adjustment. Movement can then be a useful component, but it does not replace a psychiatric or psychotherapeutic assessment.
