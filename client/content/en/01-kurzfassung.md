This compass is not a plan for perfect weeks, but a decision-making framework for normal weeks. The order is deliberate: first robust fundamentals, then optimization.

## 1.1 The most important core ideas

1. **Trends reflect daily values.** Body weight, calories, exercise performance, sleep and stress fluctuate. What matters is not a single day, but the direction over several weeks.

2. **The calorie balance controls the weight trend.** Food is rarely “good” or “bad” on its own; Quantity, regularity, satiety, environment and weekly balance count more than individual meals. Diet phase uses a moderate deficit, mass phase uses a controlled excess.

3. **Protein and food quality make the calorie balance practical.** Protein supports satiety, muscle maintenance and muscle building; For people exercising, a range of around 1.4-2.0 g/kg/day is often useful (<a href="https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8">ISSN 2017a</a>). The basis remains a predominantly plant-based, fiber-rich diet with clear protein sources, useful carbohydrates and good fat quality (<a href="https://www.dge.de/gesunde-ernaehrung/gut-essen-und-trinken/dge-empfehlungen/">DGE 2024a</a>).

4. **Carbohydrates are training energy, not an enemy.** Anyone who trains hard, runs or does a lot of intensive sessions often benefits from planning carbohydrates specifically around training and everyday life instead of avoiding them across the board.

5. **Muscle building occurs through repeated tension and subsequent protein buildup.** Mechanical tension is translated into growth signals in the muscle; mTORC1 is a central signaling pathway for muscle protein synthesis and cell growth (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12927080/">Van Every et al. 2025</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335577/">Wackerhage et al. 2019</a>). Eating more does not speed up this process indefinitely; After sufficient energy intake, the risk of unnecessary fat gain increases.

6. **Training must be hard enough, but controllable.** Progression, clean technique, reps in reserve, sensible set breaks and a training log are more important than maximum exhaustion every time. Drop sets, EMOMs and super sets can work, but they are tools and not mandatory.

7. **Health and regeneration are part of the plan.** Sleep, endurance, everyday exercise, stress management, mental health, pain signals and basic medical checks are not secondary topics. Targeted cardio improves cardiorespiratory fitness; VO2max is a useful but not the only decisive marker (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/">WHO 2020</a>; <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27881567/">Ross et al. 2016</a>; <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434546/">AASM/SRS 2015</a>).

8. **Longevity is the sum of major avoidable risks.** Smoking, high blood pressure, visceral fat, alcohol, lack of exercise, poor sleep and missed preventive care are bigger levers than most small tweaks. Cohorts and official health sources show: several good lifestyle factors together are associated with significantly longer lifespan and healthy lifespan (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6207481/">Li et al. 2018</a>; <a href="https://www.bmj.com/content/368/bmj.l6669">Li et al. 2020</a>; → [00 Why Be Healthy?](00-warum-gesund-sein.md)).

9. **Tracking helps, but is not a must.** Weight trends, nutrition and training can become more visible with apps or a simple logbook. The data is work value, not identity; [04 Tracking and Measurement](04-tracking.md) bundles measurement methods, formulas and limits.

10. **Source quality counts.** Official recommendations, guidelines, reviews and good position papers are stronger than individual posts, testimonials or hype. Coaches and YouTube can be practically valuable, but they are no substitute for solid source checking.

11. **Systems beat motivation.** Good habits are small, concrete and repeatable. A plan is good if it allows for a minimum version, even in everyday life, stress and fluctuating motivation.

## 1.2 Minimum starting point

- Plan and log 2 strength training sessions per week.
- Include a clear source of protein per main meal.
- Make fruit, vegetables, whole grains, legumes or potatoes your daily base.
- Evaluate the 7-day weight trend instead of individual weighing days.
- Integrate endurance and everyday exercise regularly: mostly relaxed, optionally targeted intervals if the base is stable.
- Treat sleep, stress and mood like real training factors.
- Don't put off blood pressure, waist circumference, preventive care and vaccination status forever.
- When dieting or gaining weight, track a few metrics until the direction is clear.
